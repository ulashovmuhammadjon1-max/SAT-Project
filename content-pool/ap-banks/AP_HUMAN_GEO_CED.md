# AP Human Geography — what the CED actually says

Source: `ced-source/HUMAN_GEO_ced.txt.gz`, a `pdftotext -layout` dump of the
official College Board *AP Human Geography Course and Exam Description*. Every
claim below is quoted or paraphrased from that file with the line range noted,
so it can be re-checked rather than remembered.

## Which edition this is — and no, the course has NOT been redesigned

The document is headed **"Course Framework V.1"** and carries **"© 2020 College
Board"** on every page. It is the **2020 framework: seven units, 68 topics**,
which is still the live structure of the course. The InDesign job stamp on the
page footers reads `16/04/26`, which is a re-issue date of the same framework,
not a new one — nothing in the file announces a revision, and a search for
"revised / updated / redesign / effective fall" turns up only the boilerplate
notice on page 43 that CEDs are updated periodically.

This is stated prominently because a sibling agent on **AP Statistics** found
that course had been cut from nine units to five and the brief assuming
otherwise was wrong. **Human Geography has not moved.** Seven units is correct;
68 topics is correct; the topic codes below are the current ones.

## Exam format (lines 6983–6995)

The exam is **2 hours 15 minutes**.

| Section | Question type | Number | Weighting | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Multiple-choice | **60** | 50% | **60 minutes** |
| II | Free-response | **3** (7 points each) | 50% | **75 minutes** |

Multiple choice is **five options, A–E** — confirmed by all fifteen sample MC
questions in the CED (lines 7136–7350). This is one of the few AP exams that has
kept five choices, so the banks here use five, not four.

Free-response detail (lines 7060–7085), because it shapes what an MC item in
this bank should look like:

- FRQ 1 has **no stimulus**; FRQ 2 has **one** stimulus (data, image or map);
  FRQ 3 has **two** stimuli.
- Each FRQ assesses **at least two different units**.
- At least two of the three FRQs assess **analysis across geographic scales**.
- Task verbs used: *Compare, Define, Describe, Explain*.

Unit 1 is the only light unit. Every other unit carries the same band, so a bank
of 30 questions per topic is *over*-weighted on Unit 1 relative to the exam
(7 topics × 30 = 210 questions for an 8–10% unit against Unit 2's 12 × 30 = 360
for a 12–17% unit). That is a property of a topic-indexed practice bank, not a
defect — a student practising topic 1.4 wants 30 questions on topic 1.4.

And the sentence that should govern how this bank is written:

> Approximately **30 to 40% of the multiple-choice questions will reference
> stimulus material**, including maps, tables, charts, graphs, images,
> infographics, and/or landscapes, roughly evenly divided between quantitative
> and qualitative sources.

So roughly a third of items should hang off real data. In this bank that means a
real `table=` — never a prose description of a figure.

The three **big ideas** (line 7000): Patterns and Spatial Organization (PSO),
Impacts and Interactions (IMP), Spatial Processes and Societal Change (SPS).
These are the prefixes on every enduring-understanding and essential-knowledge
code (`PSO-1.B.2`, `IMP-2.C.1`, `SPS-3.A.4`).

### What contradicts the brief and general expectation

1. **Weber's least-cost theory is NOT in this CED.** The authoring brief lists
   "von Thünen, Weber, Christaller, Rostow, Wallerstein, demographic transition,
   Burgess/Hoyt/Harris-Ullman" as the models the course requires. A
   case-insensitive search of the whole CED for `Weber`, `least cost` and
   `least-cost` returns **zero hits**. Weber's industrial location triangle was
   in the pre-2019 course and was dropped. Do not write a question that requires
   it, and do not use it as a distractor that a student is expected to recognise.
2. **Boserup is not named either** (zero hits). Topic 2.6 is
   "Malthusian Theory", and IMP-2.B.3 says "Malthusian theory *and its
   critiques*" — the critiques are the framework's hook, but no critic is named,
   so a question may test the *shape* of the critique (technology and yields
   rise with population; distribution rather than absolute supply is the
   constraint) without attributing it to a person the CED does not name.
3. **Griffin-Ford and the other regional city models are not named
   individually.** PSO-6.D.1 says only "urban models drawn from Latin America,
   Southeast Asia, and Africa". The names students see in textbooks
   (Griffin-Ford, McGee, de Blij) are absent from the CED.
4. **Core–periphery is not a CED phrase.** SPS-7.E.1 names Wallerstein's World
   System Theory, and the world-systems vocabulary (core, semi-periphery,
   periphery) is carried inside that; "core-periphery model" as a free-standing
   named model does not appear.
5. **"Gravity model" appears only once**, inside PSO-6.C.1's list of principles
   for city distribution and size — not as a migration model. Its CED home is
   Unit 6, not Unit 2.
6. **Unit 1 has no models at all.** Its content is maps, projections, data
   sources, spatial concepts, human-environment theory, scale and region. A
   Unit 1 question that leans on a named model is off-framework.
7. **Topic 4.1's, 4.3's, 4.7's and 4.8's titles are corrupted in the text
   dump** — the "Unit at a Glance" table drops them entirely. They were read off
   the topic pages instead: 4.1 Introduction to Political Geography, 4.3
   Political Power and Territoriality, 4.7 Forms of Governance, 4.8 Defining
   Devolutionary Factors. Two more titles come through the dump with a broken
   ligature — `Ef fects` for *Effects* in 2.12 and 3.8. `HUMAN_GEO_topics.json`
   carries the repaired titles.
8. **1.5 uses an en dash, not a hyphen**: "Human–Environmental Interaction".
   Kept verbatim in the topics JSON.

## The CED's own sample MC questions — the style to copy

Fifteen samples, lines 7136–7350. What they are and are not:

- **None is a bare definition.** The closest, #14 (religion paired with hearth
  region), is still a pairing task across five candidate pairs.
- Six of fifteen (#3–5 on a satellite image, #8–9 on a two-series chart, #11 on
  a choropleth map, #13 on a population table) hang off a **stimulus**, and the
  stimulus is *provided*. That is the ~30–40% the exam specification promises.
- **Set-based questions are normal**: #3–5 share one image, #8–9 share one chart.
- The characteristic verb is *explains* / *best describes* / *accounts for*, and
  the characteristic shape is **a real-world situation, then which concept it
  illustrates** (#2 Berlin Conference → superimposed boundaries; #7 Dubai golf
  courses, Dutch polders, Three Gorges → possibilism; #12 neo-Malthusian view of
  the Green Revolution).
- **#9 and #13 are the data-reasoning shape**: read two series against each
  other, or compare two countries' city-size ladders, and pick the conclusion the
  numbers support. #13's key turns on an actual computation — Shanghai 25.6 M
  against Beijing 19.6 M is not the 2:1 the rank-size rule predicts, while
  New York 18.8 M against Los Angeles 12.5 M is closer to it. That is the model
  for every `table=` question in this bank: the arithmetic must be recomputable
  from the table.

## Reading the CED text dump

`pdftotext -layout` interleaves the "SUGGESTED SKILL" sidebar into the topic
title, so a topic page opens like

```
SUGGESTED SKILL
      Data Analysis
                              TOPIC 1.3
3.B
Describe spatial patterns
presented in maps
                              The Power of
and in quantitative and
geospatial data.              Geographic Data
```

The title is the right-hand column read top to bottom — "The Power of Geographic
Data". Reading the file linearly gives "The Power of / and in quantitative and /
geospatial data. Geographic Data", which is how a title gets mangled. Every
title in `HUMAN_GEO_topics.json` was un-wrapped this way and cross-checked
against the Unit at a Glance table, which wraps differently.
