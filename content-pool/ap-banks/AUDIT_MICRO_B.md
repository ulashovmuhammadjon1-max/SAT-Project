# AP Microeconomics — topic-fit audit, pass B

Independent second read of all 36 Micro modules (1,800 questions: `t11`–`t16`,
`u2_1`–`u2_9`, `u3_1`–`u3_7`, `u4_1`–`u4_5`, `u5_1`–`u5_4`, `u6_1`–`u6_5`).

**Method.** Top-down, not keyword-driven. For each topic in turn I first wrote down what
the CED title (`src/lib/ap/courses.ts`, `MICRO_UNITS`) commits that topic to and what a
student who had studied *only* that topic would possess, then read all 50 questions and
asked of each: **could that student answer it?** A question whose central demand is
machinery the student has not been given is the defect. Mentioning a neighbouring topic's
vocabulary, or leaning on an earlier topic, was not counted against a question.

A cross-module near-duplicate scan was run afterwards only as corroboration — where the
same question appears in two modules, the module that is *not* its CED home is strong
evidence of drift. It was never used to *find* candidates.

Conservatism: where I could construct a fair reading in which the resident student could
answer the question from their own topic, I marked UNSURE, not MISFILED.

## 3. Patterns

### 3.1 The dominant pattern: the *destination* topic is clean, the *upstream* topic borrows

In every systematic case, the module that owns the content is complete and correct, and the
duplicate copy sits in a module that comes earlier in the sequence. Nothing is missing from
the bank; material is being taught twice, once prematurely. This is a copy-forward problem,
not a gap.

### 3.2 The two blocks I would act on first

**`u6_1` questions 14–29 are a sixteen-question government-intervention block inside an
efficiency topic.** This is the brief's suspicion confirmed, and it is the largest single
displacement in the bank. Four of them (q23–q26) are near-verbatim restatements of
`u2_8` q7–q10 and contain *no efficiency content whatsoever* — tax incidence is a
distributional question about who pays, not a question about surplus. Two more (q17 rent
control, q18 minimum wage) are pure policy-naming recall. Those six are the firm calls. Of
the remaining ten I marked six UNSURE and left four alone (q16, q20, q21, q28), because
those four are explicitly about total surplus and deadweight loss and so earn their place
under an efficiency heading.

**`u2_7` questions 14–20, 26–31 and 38–43 are a nineteen-question price-control block**, and
`u2_8` covers the same ground properly. I kept most of these UNSURE rather than MISFILED for
one honest reason: 2.7's own content is shortage and surplus, and "a binding ceiling creates
a shortage" *is* answerable from 2.7 alone. The six I flagged are the ones that go past
that — the surplus consequences and the black-market rationale — which need 2.6/2.8
machinery. If the goal is a clean topic split rather than a defensible one, the whole block
belongs in 2.8.

### 3.3 `u2_1` q21–q25: the clearest misfile in the bank

Five consecutive questions in the **Demand** module — market system, property rights,
economic agents, incentives, constraints — contain **not one word about demand**. They are
1.2 Resource Allocation and Economic Systems material, verbatim. This is the one finding I
would defend without qualification: it is not a boundary blur, it is a block of a different
topic's questions sitting in the wrong file. Note the direction is *backwards* (Unit 1
content in Unit 2), which is why it survives: the resident student *has* seen this material,
so nothing looks broken at exam time. It is still not a demand question.

### 3.4 The 3.5 / 3.6 boundary, and why I would not act on it blind

Eleven `u3_5` questions turn on the shutdown rule (P vs minimum AVC), which the CED assigns
to 3.6 alongside the long-run exit decision, and `u3_6` already covers it (q10, q12, q32,
q33, q43, q49). I have marked all eleven UNSURE rather than MISFILED because most textbook
treatments bundle shutdown with profit maximization, and because a 3.5 student who has met
ATC/AVC can reason the rule out. **This is the single item where a human decision about
scope would settle 11 questions at once**, and it is worth making that decision explicitly
before anything is moved.

### 3.5 The `u3_5` / `u3_7` overlap is real but is *not* drift

`u3_5` q5, q6, q7, q21, q29, q30, q31 restate perfect-competition characteristics that
`u3_7` also covers. I flagged none of them. A profit-maximization topic *must* establish
P = MR for a price taker before MR = MC becomes P = MC; the questions are load-bearing where
they sit. This is the case the brief warned about, and after reading it I think 3.5/3.7 is
in good shape — the genuine 3.x problem is 3.5/3.6, not 3.5/3.7.

### 3.6 One title discrepancy (not a misfile)

`u3_6` declares `TOPIC = ("3.6", "Firms' Entry and Exit Decisions in the Long Run", 3)`.
The authoritative title in `src/lib/ap/courses.ts` is **"Firms' Entry and Exit Decisions"**
— no "in the Long Run". Worth aligning, and the appended phrase may itself be what nudged
the short-run shutdown material out of this module and into 3.5. Every other module's
`(code, title, unit)` triple matches the CED exactly.

Recorded so a third reader knows these were considered and cleared, not missed:

  — they are structurally parallel with different numbers, and MRP is legitimately 5.1's.

### 3.8 A note on the duplicate scan

The near-duplicate scan produced 143 pairs above 0.62 Jaccard, of which the large majority
are **false signals from shared table-question phrasing** ("Using the table, Firm B is…"
matches "Using the table, Firm L is…" at 1.00 while testing entirely different content). I
used it only to corroborate findings already made by reading. Two pairs it surfaced were
genuinely diagnostic and are in the table above: `u3_3` q19 ≡ `u4_2` q27 (byte-identical
natural-monopoly stem) and `u2_3` q33 ≡ `u2_4` q22 (the same tax-incidence question written
from each side). Consistent with the standing lesson in `CLAUDE.md`: a similarity score
decides what to read, never what to accept.
