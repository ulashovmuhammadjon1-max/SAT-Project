# Shorts playbook: what the numbers say, and the rules for the next video

Read this before making the next scholarly.space Short. It comes from the Instagram insights of the first two SAT
question Reels, as the owner sent them in screenshots. The retention figures were read off Instagram's
"How long people watched your reel" graph, so they are approximate (±3 points).

**Don't trust likes, comments, reposts or saves on these Reels.** Most of them came from the owner's own accounts
(on Short #2, 1 of the 6 comments was a stranger). The real signals are watch time, the retention curve, views
per viewer, and where the views came from.

## The data

| | Short #1 `video/sat-trap` (x + 1/x = 5) | Short #2 v4 `video/sat-hardest` (3^(2s) = 7) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 32.4 s | 32.8 s |
| Views | 623 at ~70 min | 1,537 (1,081 viewers) at ~3 h |
| Average watch time | 7 s | **10 s** |
| Still watching at 3 s | ~58% | ~52% |
| Still watching at 5 s | ~49% | ~42% |
| Just before the answer | ~24% (at 26 s) | ~27% (at 23 s) |
| ~3 s after the answer | ~11% | **~7%** |
| Saw "Join the community" | — | ~5% |
| Follows | 1 (at ~1.7k views) | 1 (at ~2k views) |
| Profile visits | 1 | — |
| Views from Reels tab / Explore / Feed / Profile | 87.0 / 6.3 / 2.1 / 0.4 % | 89.3 / 8.6 / 0.7 / 0.2 % |

What it shows:
- **The middle works.** From 5 s to the answer, Short #1 lost half of those still watching and Short #2 only about
  a third. The 3-2-1 countdown causes **no drop** in either video, so keep it.
- **People rewatch.** On Short #2, 1,537 views came from 1,081 viewers, about 1.4 views each. A short tail after
  the answer, looping back to the question, feeds this.
- **The reveal is the like moment.** On Short #1, likes peaked at ~26 s, exactly when B was circled with the ding
  and the student's jump. About 30% of its likes came at 0:00, before anyone could have watched; that's people who
  already know the account.
- **The first 3 seconds lose about half the audience,** and the smoother v4 opening didn't help. It may have hurt,
  because the camera glide starts wide, so the question is smallest exactly when people decide whether to stay.
- **Once the answer is given, people leave.** About two-thirds of the remaining viewers are gone within ~3 s of
  it. Only ~7% saw the scholarly.space end card and ~5% heard "Join the community", while ~35% were still watching
  during the countdown.
- **About 1 follow per ~2,000 views.** Nothing in the video gives a reason to come back, and the views come almost entirely from
  the Reels tab (people who don't follow), not from the profile.

## Rules for the next Short

1. **Open on the question, close and big, from frame 0.** Start the voice at 0.0 s. Keep the music and a whoosh on
   frame 0 so it doesn't feel abrupt (that was the owner's complaint about v3), but no slow glide from a wide shot.
2. **Put scholarly.space in the countdown, not at the end.** Show it as on-screen text during the 3-2-1, e.g.
   "From Test 29 · free on scholarly.space". That costs no voice credits and reaches 5–7× more viewers than the
   end card.
3. **End within ~2 s of the answer:**
   - the reveal (ding, circle, jump),
   - then "Join the community!" plus on-screen "Follow for #N+1",
   - then loop back to the question.

   No long promo after the answer.
4. **Make it a numbered series** ("Hardest SAT math question #3"), which gives people a reason to follow.
5. **Keep what works:**
   - the 3-2-1 countdown with the ticking clock and the music lift,
   - a step label for every move on the board,
   - the ding and jump on the answer,
   - the music bed,
   - the Liam voice (`video/voice/README.md`).
6. **Aim for ~25 s.** That's the owner's original ask, and it falls out naturally once the tail is cut.

## When posting
- **Set up the profile first:** make the bio link scholarly.space, and pin a comment such as "Answer + 31 free SAT
  practice tests: link in bio 👆".
- **Reply to every genuine comment,** ending with a question (e.g. "Did you try to solve for s first?"). A
  back-and-forth counts for more than a single comment.
- **No likes, comments, saves or reposts from the owner's own accounts.** They make the numbers unreadable.
- **The day after,** check the Non-followers tab and the retention graph, and add the numbers to the table above.
- **Cross-post the same file to YouTube Shorts and TikTok.** Never repost it to Instagram, where a duplicate
  competes with the original.
